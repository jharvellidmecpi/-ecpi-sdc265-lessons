Trace and Write a Plain-Language Algorithm for a “Battle Run”

An algorithm is a set of instructions. A plain-language algorithm describes how to solve a problem using ordinary words. A battle run is one sequence in which two characters take turns attacking until one wins.

A good algorithm is: Ordered (clear sequence), Specific (exact steps), Repeatable (same result for anyone), Conditional (depends on what happens), and Complete (clear beginning, middle, end).

Weak algorithm

“The hero fights the monster. They attack each other until someone wins.”

Too vague: who attacks first? How is damage calculated? When is health checked? When does it end? It could run forever, and the cloud bill comes every month.

Strong algorithm

Click each step to mark it as Ordered, Conditional, or Repeated.

Examine the difference

The strong algorithm meets all five qualities. You know who attacks first, how health drops, when defeat is checked, and when the battle stops. You just follow the steps until someone runs out of health.

Push your thinking: Which steps are Boolean logic? Which are repetition? Which are conditions? (Use the Strong tab to explore.)
