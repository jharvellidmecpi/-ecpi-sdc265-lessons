Review: What Counts as One Step?

For any algorithmic analysis to succeed, you have to define what counts as a step and keep that rule the whole way through.

Activity 2: What If the Slime Has More Health?

As the shampoo bottle once told us, “Lather, rinse, repeat.” Now change the Slime’s health from 18 to 30. The Knight still has 25 health and 6 attack, and the Slime still has 3 attack.

Here’s a handy step-estimating technique: the Slime loses 6 health each time the Knight attacks. If the Slime starts with 30 health, about how many Knight attacks are needed? If the division doesn’t come out evenly, round up. A character can’t take half an attack. More Knight attacks usually also means more Slime counterattacks and more health checks.

Final thoughts

Steps add to costs, so pay attention to how the instructions you program into an algorithm contribute. Counting steps by hand may feel tedious, but it teaches an important lesson: algorithms do work. When an algorithm runs once, the cost may seem small. When it runs thousands or millions of times, small costs add up to high costs. That’s the beginning of efficiency thinking.
